# Together

## Vision Document

**Working domain:** together.ng  
**Status:** Vision / product philosophy  
**Origin:** Nigeria, with the ambition to grow beyond Nigeria

---

# 1. The Vision

**Together is a non-monetary network for people to help one another become more capable.**

People have knowledge, skills, books, tools, equipment, experience, time, access, and institutional knowledge that other people need. Much of this capacity is unused or difficult to discover.

Together exists to connect those needs with people who are willing and able to help.

A person should be able to say:

> **“I need help becoming better at this.”**

without feeling ashamed, without having to beg, and without owing the person who helps them.

Likewise, someone should be able to say:

> **“I can help with this.”**

and easily discover people who could benefit from what they know or have.

Together is built around a simple idea:

> **Help received does not create a debt to the giver. It creates an opportunity for the receiver to eventually help someone else.**

---

# 2. The Problem

There is enormous human capacity in the world that is poorly coordinated.

People have:

- Knowledge they could teach
- Skills they could share
- Books they no longer use
- Tools that sit idle
- Equipment that could help someone learn or build
- Professional experience
- Mentorship they could provide
- Institutional and local knowledge
- Access to resources that others cannot easily find
- Time they are willing to contribute

At the same time, people encounter barriers that prevent them from learning, building, creating, or progressing.

Someone may need:

- A book
- A mentor
- An explanation
- Feedback
- A tool
- Temporary access to equipment
- Guidance from someone experienced
- A learning resource
- Help navigating an unfamiliar process
- Someone nearby who knows how to do something
- Someone far away with specialized knowledge

Often, the resource exists. The person who can help exists.

**The problem is that they cannot find each other.**

Together is intended to become a coordination layer between these two sides.

---

# 3. What Together Is Not

The distinction between Together and other forms of assistance is fundamental.

### Together is not a begging platform.

People should not have to frame their needs as pleas for charity.

The platform should encourage requests that help people **learn, build, create, solve problems, and increase their future capabilities.**

### Together is not a marketplace.

There is no requirement that help be exchanged for money.

There is no marketplace price for knowledge, mentorship, books, tools, or skills.

### Together is not a barter system.

A person who receives help does not owe an equivalent amount of help to the person who provided it.

### Together is not a popularity contest.

Followers, likes, wealth, and social status should not determine someone's value.

### Together is not a charity ranking system.

A person's dignity should not depend on how much they have to give.

A person may join Together with nothing to offer and still be fully welcome.

---

# 4. The Core Principle: Capability Over Consumption

Together should prioritize requests that increase a person's ability to do something.

There is an important difference between:

> “I need money.”

and:

> “I want to learn software development but don't have access to a computer.”

The first is primarily a request for consumption.

The second is a request for access to a capability-building resource.

Together is primarily concerned with the second kind of problem.

This does not mean people's immediate needs are unimportant. It means Together has a specific mission:

> **Reduce the barriers that prevent people from learning, building, creating, and becoming more capable.**

---

# 5. Asking for Help Is a Feature, Not a Failure

One of Together's most important cultural principles is that **asking for help should carry no shame.**

A person should be able to join the platform without having anything to contribute.

They should be able to ask for help without being treated as inferior.

There should be no expectation that someone must first prove their usefulness before receiving assistance.

Together should communicate:

> **You are allowed to need help.**

People become capable through other people.

A student learns from a teacher.

An apprentice learns from a craftsperson.

An engineer learns from another engineer.

A founder learns from someone who has done it before.

A person who receives help today may become the person helping others tomorrow.

---

# 6. The Network Is Not an Exchange

Together should deliberately avoid creating a system where every act of help creates an obligation.

If Alice helps Bob learn Python, Bob does not owe Alice an equivalent amount of help.

If Alice gives Bob a book, Bob does not owe Alice a book.

If Alice lends Bob a camera, Bob does not gain a debt measured in points or credits.

Instead:

**Alice → Bob → Charlie → David**

Help flows through the network.

The social principle is:

> **Pay it forward, not pay it back.**

A person can contribute whenever they are able.

This creates the possibility of a compounding network of generosity and capability.

# 7. What People Can Ask For

Together should support many forms of productive help.

### Knowledge

- Explanations
- Research guidance
- Local knowledge
- Institutional knowledge
- Practical knowledge
- Advice from experience

### Education

- Mentorship
- Tutoring
- Study groups
- Learning resources
- Guidance on what to learn
- Feedback on learning projects

### Skills

- Learning a skill
- Teaching a skill
- Reviewing someone's work
- Pairing or collaborating
- Practical instruction

### Resources

- Books
- Tools
- Equipment
- Devices
- Educational materials
- Other useful physical or digital resources

### Access

Sometimes the scarce resource is not an object but access to something.

Examples include:

- A laboratory
- Workshop equipment
- Specialized software
- Professional communities
- Institutions
- People with relevant experience

### Collaboration

People may need others to help them:

- Build something
- Research something
- Test something
- Create something
- Solve a difficult problem
- Start a project

The common thread is not the category.

The common thread is:

> **Does this help someone move forward?**

# 8. Asking Well

A major feature of Together should be helping people formulate useful requests.

A vague request such as:

> “I need a laptop.”

does not provide enough information for the community or matching system.

Together can guide the person toward something like:

> “I'm learning backend development and currently only have access to my phone. I need a computer capable of running VS Code and Docker so I can complete a 12-week course. I live in Ibadan and can pick it up locally.”

A good request communicates:

- What the person is trying to accomplish
- What is preventing them from progressing
- What kind of help would solve the problem
- Relevant constraints
- Location when relevant
- Duration when relevant
- The desired outcome

Better requests improve both **human discovery** and **automated matching**.

Together should help users turn:

> **“I need help.”**

into:

> **“This is what I'm trying to accomplish, this is the barrier, and this is the specific help that would move me forward.”**

---

# 9. Matching

When users register, they can specify areas in which they are willing to help.

These should initially come from a predefined taxonomy of categories.

Users can then provide more specific capabilities within those categories.

For example:

**Category:** Software Development

**Can help with:**

- JavaScript
- Python
- Backend architecture
- PostgreSQL
- Linux
- Code review

A person does not need to offer anything.

They can leave their contribution profile empty and simply participate as someone seeking help.

When a new request is created, Together can identify people whose stated capabilities are relevant.

Potential contributors can receive notifications when requests matching their interests appear.

Matching should eventually consider:

- Skills
- Resource type
- Location
- Availability
- Duration
- User preferences
- Request quality
- Previous successful contributions
- Other relevant context

The goal is not to maximize notifications.

The goal is to maximize **useful connections.**

---

# 10. Discovery Beyond Matching

Not every contribution will happen through automated matching.

Together should provide public discovery surfaces.

There can be:

### Featured Needs

A general feed of requests that the community can discover.

### Category Feeds

Requests within areas such as:

- Education
- Technology
- Science
- Arts
- Engineering
- Business
- Trades
- Books
- Tools
- etc.

### Voting

People can upvote requests they want to see receive attention.

An upvote does not represent money, reputation currency, or an obligation.

It can simply mean:

> **“I think this is worth helping.”**

This creates a second way of contributing.

Someone who cannot personally satisfy a request can still increase its visibility.

The ranking system should eventually combine factors such as:

- Community interest
- Recency
- Request quality
- Category relevance
- Potential matches
- Urgency

rather than relying solely on popularity.

---

# 11. Reputation Without Currency

Together should have reputation, but reputation should never become money.

There should be no:

- Coins
- Credits
- Points that represent exchange value
- Follower counts
- Pay-to-increase reputation

Instead, reputation should describe a person's history of contribution and trust.

Potential signals include:

### Contributor since

When the person joined the contributing community.

### People helped

The number of people who report successfully receiving help.

### Contributions

A record of completed contributions.

### Verified skills

Skills for which there is meaningful evidence or community verification.

### Trust signals

Evidence of reliable participation.

### Badges

Badges can recognize patterns of contribution without becoming a currency.

Examples:

- Helpful Mentor
- Book Sharer
- Community Contributor
- Skill Contributor
- Resource Lender
- Consistent Contributor
- Early Contributor

Badges should celebrate behavior rather than create a competitive hierarchy.

The objective is:

> **Trust, not status.**

---

# 12. Everyone Can Be a Receiver and a Contributor

Together should avoid creating a permanent distinction between:

**people who give**

and

**people who receive.**

People are likely to move between these roles throughout their lives.

Someone may join with several needs and no contributions.

Later they may learn something.

Then they can teach it.

Someone may have knowledge but no physical resources.

Someone else may have resources but little specialized knowledge.

Someone may be highly skilled professionally but need help with something completely unrelated.

This is what makes the network fundamentally different from charity.

> **Everyone has something they need, and almost everyone has something they can eventually contribute.**

---

# 13. Physical Resources and Lending

Physical resources introduce additional trust and safety concerns.

Together should treat lending differently from low-risk forms of help such as advice or knowledge sharing.

Examples include:

- Books
- Tools
- Electronics
- Cameras
- Computers
- Musical instruments
- Workshop equipment

Risk should increase with the value and sensitivity of the resource.

Possible safeguards include:

- Progressive identity verification
- Trust requirements for higher-value lending
- Lending history
- Clear lending agreements
- Photos documenting condition before handoff
- Photos documenting condition on return
- Both parties confirming handoff and return
- Reporting and dispute mechanisms
- Restrictions on newly created accounts accessing high-value resources

A new account may be able to ask:

> “Can someone teach me Python?”

without being allowed to immediately borrow an expensive camera.

Trust should be earned through behavior.

---

# 14. Accountability

Reputation is useful, but reputation alone is not sufficient protection.

Someone who intentionally damages or steals a resource should not simply be able to delete an account and start again.

Together should therefore distinguish between:

**Public reputation**

and

**internal trust and safety records.**

For serious violations, Together can:

- Suspend accounts
- Restrict access to lending
- Require additional verification
- Prevent new accounts from immediately accessing high-risk activities
- Investigate disputes
- Preserve relevant incident records
- Cooperate with appropriate authorities where legally required

The platform should prefer **accountability over public shaming.**

The goal is to protect the community rather than create a public punishment system.

---

# 15. Insurance

Insurance should not be a requirement for the basic Together experience.

The initial platform should focus on building the community and validating whether people actually want to share resources.

If physical lending becomes significant, Together could eventually explore partnerships with insurance providers for higher-value resources.

This could become an optional layer:

**Community lending**  
For low-risk resources and trusted participants.

**Protected lending**  
For higher-value resources where additional protection is useful.

Insurance is therefore infrastructure that may emerge as the network grows, rather than the foundation of the product.

---

Organizations may have enormous amounts of unused capacity.

Potential contributors include:

- Schools

- Workshops

An institution might provide:

> **people ↔ people ↔ institutions ↔ resources**

# 17. The Human Capability Graph

Over time, Together could develop into more than a collection of requests.

It could become a graph connecting:

**People**  
↓  
**Skills**  
↓  
**Resources**  
↓  
**Projects**  
↓  
**Needs**  
↓  
**People**

For example:

A person wants to learn electronics.

They find a mentor.

The mentor recommends a book.

Someone nearby has the book.

Another person has a soldering station.

The learner builds a project.

They eventually acquire enough knowledge to help another beginner.

This creates a network where **knowledge, skills, resources, and people continuously connect.**

The long-term opportunity is not merely helping people find answers.

It is making human capability more discoverable.

---

# 18. AI as a Coordination Layer

AI can eventually make Together substantially more useful, but AI should not replace the human contribution at the heart of the network.

AI can help users:

- Formulate better requests
- Identify what they actually need
- Break large goals into specific needs
- Match requests to skills and resources
- Recommend relevant contributors
- Recommend relevant resources
- Summarize or structure requests
- Detect duplicate or related requests
- Improve search and discovery
- Help contributors understand how they can help

For example, a user might say:

> “I want to learn electronics but I don't know where to start.”

Together could transform this into:

**Goal:** Learn basic electronics  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Needs:** Learning path + mentor + practical resources  
**Location:** Ibadan  
**Desired outcome:** Build a first working circuit

It can then search the network for people, books, tools, institutions, and other resources.

AI becomes the **coordination layer**.

Humans remain the source of many of the scarce things that matter:

- Experience
- Trust
- Mentorship
- Physical resources
- Local knowledge
- Access
- Relationships
- Judgment

# 19. The Product Flywheel

Together's ideal cycle is:

**A person has a goal**  
↓  
**They encounter a barrier**  
↓  
**They ask for specific help**  
↓  
**Together finds relevant people/resources**  
↓  
**Someone contributes**  
↓  
**The person progresses**  
↓  
**The contribution is recorded as successful**  
↓  
**Trust increases**  
↓  
**The recipient becomes more capable**  
↓  
**They eventually contribute to someone else**

The most important transition is:

> **Need → Capability → Contribution**

That is the behavior Together wants to encourage.

# 20. What Success Looks Like

Together should not primarily measure success through:

- Registered users
- Followers
- Page views
- Likes

Those are secondary.

The core metric should be something closer to:

> **Successful contributions.**

A successful contribution occurs when:

1. Someone identifies a meaningful need.
2. Another person or organization responds.
3. The help actually happens.
4. The recipient confirms that it helped them move forward.

Important supporting metrics include:

- Requests created
- Requests receiving responses
- Match rate
- Successful contributions
- Time to first useful response
- Repeat contributors
- Repeat recipients
- Resource lending completion rate
- Percentage of recipients who later contribute
- Category-level activity

A small community with thousands of successful contributions is more valuable than a large community with little meaningful interaction.

# 21. Product Principles

Together should be guided by several principles.

### Dignity first

Asking for help should never be treated as shameful.

### Capability over consumption

Prioritize help that enables people to learn, build, create, and progress.

### Contribution without debt

Receiving help never creates an obligation to repay the giver.

### Trust over popularity

Reputation should communicate reliability and contribution, not social status.

### Human contribution over transactional value

The platform should make it easier for people to contribute things that markets often fail to coordinate.

### Specificity improves generosity

Better-defined needs make it easier for people to recognize that they can help.

### Everyone belongs

A person can contribute nothing at first and still be welcome.

### Start open, become trusted

Low-risk participation should remain accessible while higher-risk activities require stronger trust.

### Attention is also contribution

Helping a useful request become visible can itself be valuable.

### The platform should facilitate, not dominate

Together should coordinate people rather than make itself the center of every interaction.

# 22. The Long-Term Vision

The long-term vision for Together is a world where **access to human capability is less constrained by money, geography, or the limits of one's immediate social circle.**

A person should not have to already know the right person to find:

- A teacher
- A mentor
- A book
- A tool
- A skill
- An explanation
- A collaborator
- A local expert
- A useful resource
- Someone willing to help

The internet has connected people socially.

Marketplaces have connected people economically.

Search engines have connected people to information.

Together aims to help connect people to **each other's capacity to help.**

Not through money.

Not through debt.

Not through status.

Through contribution.

> **Together is a network for helping people become more capable.**
>
> **Ask for what you need to learn, build, create, or progress. Share what you know, what you can do, or what you have.**
>
> **You don't have to pay back the person who helped you. When you're able, help someone else.**
>
> **Together, we make human capability easier to find and easier to share.**
